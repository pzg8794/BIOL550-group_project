Methods used

This report keeps the same notebook and derived-analysis framework from previous weeks, but the emphasis here is on interpretation rather than on generating additional contrasts. Starting from the bend-point-selected core for ipsi_vs_contra_in_ff, I split the genes by the sign of log2FoldChange, re-used the direction-split g:Profiler outputs, and created a compact anchor-gene table by selecting the top 5 upregulated and top 5 downregulated genes ranked by adjusted p-value. The goal was to make the GO discussion easier to explain statistically while keeping the side-specific branch as the lead story.

Problems encountered

The main challenge this week was not computation but explanation. GO output is still highly redundant, and the direction split made the asymmetry of the FF branch more obvious: most of the enrichment signal is carried by the upregulated genes, while the downregulated side remains much weaker. That makes it easy to overstate pathway labels if we do not keep a gene-level companion in view.

The anchor-gene table helps with that, but it is still only a discussion aid. We can explain it statistically, and it gives us something concrete to bring to the team, but it does not replace the need to decide which biological themes are actually worth carrying into the draft.
