New biological interpretation

The ShinyGO-style filter collapses all 20 shortlisted FF upregulated terms into a single coherent group with cell migration as the representative term (best FDR 9.9×10−33). This means the FF injury-side signal is not diffuse — it reflects one dominant gene program spanning signaling, stress response, neurogenesis, and cell movement. The anchor genes driving this program across multiple terms are Atf3, Gadd45a, Jun, Sox11, and Flrt3 — all established injury-response or regeneration markers in DRG contexts, which fits the 1 dpi spinal cord injury design.

The FF vs CRE comparison shows that 87.5% of the FF bend-point genes (620 of 709) are also present in the CRE bend-point set, and 14 of the 20 top GO terms are shared. This convergence means the core injury-response program is genotype-independent. The divergence — 250 cKO-only genes driving phosphorylation and phosphate-containing compound metabolic process — is the cleanest genotype-specific signal we have and is the right focus for the Discussion's genotype paragraph.

Methods used

Both bend-point gene sets (ipsi_vs_contra_in_ff: 709 genes; ipsi_vs_contra_in_cre: 870 genes) were submitted to the g:Profiler API (mmusculus, GO:BP source, FDR threshold 0.05) with evidence and gene membership retained. A ShinyGO-style redundancy filter was applied: terms were clustered by shared-gene overlap, the most significant term per cluster was kept, and survivors were re-sorted by fold enrichment. This reduced 370 FF upregulated GO:BP terms to 20 shortlisted terms. The same procedure was mirrored for CRE. Network views were built by linking terms that share ≥20% of genes or ≥3 genes outright. Gene membership was traced back from the top terms to identify anchor genes supporting the strongest signals.

Problems encountered

The upregulated and downregulated GO branches are structurally asymmetric: the upregulated query returns 370 terms with strong signal; the downregulated branch returns only 2 weakly significant terms. This is real biology, not an analysis artifact — the ipsilateral injury side at 1 dpi activates a much broader transcriptional program than it represses. The report treats the upregulated branch as the primary interpretive path and keeps the downregulated branch as secondary context only, which accurately reflects the signal strength.

A second challenge was deciding what not to include. The notebook generates chart, tree, network, groups, and gene-membership views. For a weekly report, including all of them would obscure rather than clarify the biology. The selection here — the overlap table, the fold-enrichment chart, and the key anchor genes — is the minimal set that supports a professor-facing conversation about which GO themes are strongest and what to write in the Discussion.
